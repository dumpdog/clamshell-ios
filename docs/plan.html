Build plan · iPhone Duo · 25 Oct 2026

Clamshell

Tiny games you play by folding your phone. Snap it shut to chomp a burger, pump it like an accordion, hold a drawbridge at exactly 40°. Each game lasts five seconds, and every run ends with a clip ready to post.

The pick:

Of 15 ideas, a fold-controlled party game scored highest (8.4/10). It's the only kind of app that needs the hinge to work at all, it reads in a 6-second video with no explanation, and several runner-up ideas fit inside it as extra games. A starter Xcode project with 4 playable games is ready in clamshell/.

Ground truth

What the iPhone Duo actually is

The Duo isn't a rumor. Apple announced it on 9 September 2026, and it shipped on 23 October running iOS 27.1. The facts below come from Apple's newsroom, the MacRumors roundup, and Apple's four iPhone Duo developer tech talks.

Assumptions I'm making
  • API names are as Apple showed them in the talks. I couldn't check SDK headers from here, so every Duo-only call is in one file (DuoSupport.swift) behind a DUO_SDK flag.
  • The hinge angle updates smoothly, at least 30 times a second, while the phone is moving. Apple's guitar pitch-bend demo suggests it does.
  • About 0° is closed and about 180° is flat. The code learns the real range as it goes.
  • Third-party apps can show content on the outer screen while the phone is open only through scene accessories such as CameraCaptureAccessory, which needs an active camera session. That rules out a true two-player, two-screen game.
Brainstorm

15 ideas, scored

Each idea gets 1 to 10 on five axes. The total is weighted toward virality because that was the brief: Viral 35%, Feasible for one developer in 6 weeks 20%, Retention (do people come back) 15%, Defensible (won't be copied or built into iOS by Apple) 15%, Money 15%.

Popularity is my forecast of how far each idea spreads beyond Duo owners: Breakout means it can reach a general TikTok audience, Strong means a loyal Duo niche plus press, and Niche means it's useful but nobody shares it.

Why Clamshell wins

  • Only possible on a fold. Most Duo apps are just a bigger layout. Here the hinge is the game, so Apple has a reason to feature it.
  • Reads without sound. Someone biting a burger with a $2,000 phone is understood in one second.
  • Built-in sharing. ReplayKit keeps the last 15 seconds on hand. One tap after a run, and the clip is ready.
  • Absorbs the runners-up. The accordion, the combination lock and the clapperboard all become micro-games.
  • Tilt mode. Friends without a Duo can play by tilting their phone, so every share can turn into an install.

Strong runners-up

  • Fold Pet (7.35): a creature that lives in the hinge. Closing the phone tucks it into bed, and it sleeps in StandBy. It retains better than Clamshell but spreads more slowly. It's the best version 2 to add, or a cosmetic mascot.
  • Two-Face Interview Cam (7.0): street-interview tool. The question card and a live preview face the guest on the outer screen. Creators would pay for it, but Apple's Duo Preview already covers half of it.
The product

How Clamshell plays

A run works like WarioWare. A random micro-game flashes its verb ("CHOMP!") for under a second, then you have 3 to 8 seconds to do it with the fold. Win and the next one is faster. Every fourth win raises the speed. You have four lives. A run lasts 1 to 3 minutes, which is the right length for a clip and for passing the phone around.

Modes

  • Solo run: endless, with a Game Center leaderboard.
  • Daily Fold: the same seeded 10-game run for everyone, once a day. It ends with a shareable result grid (🟩🟥🟩🟩…), the way Wordle does.
  • Party Mode: pass and play. On a Duo, the outer screen shows the scoreboard to the room while the inner camera records reactions.
  • Tilt Mode: any iPhone running iOS 26 can play by tilting.

What makes it feel good

  • Input to screen in under 30 ms. The fold has to feel like the jaw.
  • Core Haptics on every bite, click and pop, tuned per game.
  • Bold, loud art that reads on a phone screen inside a TikTok.
  • No slamming. Given the hinge-crack reports, wins reward control, not force. See Risks.
Engineering

Architecture

A single SwiftUI app. Games never touch the hinge API. They read a normalized HingeFrame (openness 0 to 1, velocity, dt), so the same game runs on the real hinge, on tilt, or on a debug slider.

Key decisions

  • Canvas instead of SpriteKit for version 1. Each game is around 100 lines of drawing, with no scene graph. If a game needs physics (Catapult, Pancake), move that one to SpriteKit inside a SpriteView.
  • Canonical stage. Games always draw with a horizontal fold, and the stage view rotates the canvas when the device is in book pose.
  • Per-frame state isn't observed. Games and the clock are @ObservationIgnored, so SwiftUI only rebuilds views when the phase changes.
  • One Duo file. If an SDK name differs from the talks, only DuoSupport.swift needs fixing.

Business

Monetization

Model: free to try, pay once

  • Free: 6 games, solo runs, the Daily Fold, Tilt Mode.
  • Full Pack, $4.99 one time: all games, Party Mode, every future game. People who paid $2,000 for a phone buy a $5 toy without thinking. Party games also sell better without a subscription.
  • Skins, $0.99–$1.99: monster jaws, a retro handheld shell, a seasonal theme.
  • No ads. They'd ruin clips, and the audience is premium.

Rough math (assumptions, not data)

  • Suppose 10 million Duos are sold by mid-2027, a guess based on a $2,000 launch.
  • 3% install it, about 300,000 people. Tilt Mode adds non-Duo installs from clips that spread.
  • 8% of installs buy the Full Pack at $4.99, about $120,000, plus skins, before Apple's 15% small-business cut.
  • The upside is one breakout clip. A single viral "phone eats burger" video can push installs well past 3%.
Go to market

Launch and viral strategy

Built into the product

  • Clip in one tap after every run, with a reaction cam picture-in-picture, a small "Clamshell" watermark and an end card that says "Play it on any iPhone."
  • Daily Fold result grid that can be pasted into group chats.
  • Party Mode: the outer screen faces the room, so everyone in the room watches and wants a turn.
  • Tilt Mode means a share never dead-ends with "you need a Duo."

Outside the product

  • Hero clip: "My $2,000 phone ate my lunch." Chomp with a real burger behind the phone.
  • Seeding: 20 tech creators who already posted Duo unboxings get TestFlight a week early. Their audience owns, or wants, a Duo.
  • Apple pitch: submit to App Store editorial and the "Apps for iPhone Duo" collection. Apple needs showcase apps that use the hinge.
  • Press angle: "The first game where the fold is the controller," pitched to MacRumors, 9to5Mac and The Verge, the way the Walkman app got covered.
  • #ChompChallenge: best Daily Fold score each week wins a skin.
  • Product Hunt and r/iphone on launch day, with a dev-diary thread on X and Threads.
Risks

What could sink it

RiskMitigation
Hinge wear and "Crackgate." A game that makes people fold a $2,000 phone hundreds of times could get blamed for cracked screens.Cap pump games at about 8 folds. Reward controlled motion over force. Show a gentle "no slamming" note on first run. Track folds per session and add a cooldown.
API names or behavior differ from the tech talksAll Duo calls sit in one file. Day 1 on the device is spent calibrating.
Hinge updates are too slow for fast gamesInterpolate between samples, lean on hold and slow games, and widen timing windows.
Apple builds fold games into iOS, or a big studio copies the ideaShip first and ship content every two weeks. The brand and the daily habit are the moat.
App Review rejects the outer-screen scoreboardIt's a Party Mode extra, with a real camera recording. Clamshell works fully without it.
Small install baseTilt Mode on every iPhone running iOS 26. The Duo is the hook, not the limit.
Tomorrow

First hour on the Mac

  1. Install Xcode 27.1 and open clamshell/Clamshell.xcodeproj. If it won't open, run xcodegen.
  2. Set your Team and bundle ID. Run on any simulator in Slider mode to check the core loop.
  3. Add DUO_SDK to Active Compilation Conditions, then fix any API name mismatches in DuoSupport.swift.
  4. Run on the iPhone Duo simulator in Device Hub and confirm the menu says "Fold detected."
  5. Run on your Duo and play Chomp 20 times. Write down what feels laggy. That's the M1 to-do list.

The code has never been compiled. I had no Mac or Swift compiler tonight, so expect a handful of small fixes on the first build. The README lists the likely spots.
